# Content

## 1. Shot Sequence Design Principles

### 1.1 Breathing Rhythm of Shot Scale

Treat shot-scale transitions (wide -> close-up) as a tool for controlling the audience's "psychological distance":
- **Steady breathing**: Adjacent shots transition gently (full -> medium), creating a calm, objective atmosphere
- **Rapid breathing**: Shot scale jumps quickly (full -> close-up) or uses consecutive short close-ups, creating tension and subjective experience

Principles:
- Avoid more than two consecutive shots at the same scale (unless there's a clear narrative purpose)
- Every scale change must have an emotional or narrative motivation

### 1.2 Information Increment Rhythm

Each shot is an "information packet" - manage audience curiosity by controlling reveal and concealment:
- **Saturation-point switching**: Switch right after the audience absorbs the information (e.g., character looks at phone -> finishes reading -> cut to reaction)
- **Suspense chains**: Control the presentation order (hand on door handle -> door opens -> character looks into room -> ...each step leaves one question)

Principles:
- Avoid "information dead-end" shots (revealing everything at once)
- Use shot sequences to present different dimensions of information in order

### 1.3 Time Compression & Expansion

Treat time as a malleable material:
- **Dead-time compression**: Aggressively compress meaningless transitions (3-minute preparation -> three 1.5s close-ups)
- **Highlight expansion**: Multi-angle, slow-motion deconstruction of key moments

## 2. Camera Movement Narrative Functions

- **Push In**: Guide attention, enter inner world, intensify emotion
- **Pull Out**: Reveal context, establish space, create distance
- **Tracking**: Accompany the character, create flow, maintain action continuity
- **Orbit**: Showcase full view, dynamic beauty, emphasize subject
- **Pan/Tilt**: Scan environment, establish spatial continuity

Stability styles:
- **Stabilizer/Gimbal**: Smooth, elegant, cinematic
- **Handheld**: Energetic, subjective, documentary feel
- **Locked-off**: Objective, steady, observational

## 3. Shot Connection Logic

### Continuity Connections (Creating Realism)
- **Action match**: Action continues seamlessly from shot A -> B
- **Eyeline match**: Gaze direction in A is answered in B
- **Cause-and-effect**: An action/sound in A becomes the reason to cut to B

### Rhetorical Connections (Creating Meaning)
- **Parallel montage**: Two storylines in different times/spaces alternate, creating contrast/symbolism
- **Cross-cutting**: Two simultaneous storylines alternate rapidly, building suspense
- **Metaphorical montage**: Juxtapose unrelated subjects to create symbolism
- **Accumulation montage**: A series of progressive short shots compressing time
- **Repetition montage**: Repeat similar shots to reinforce theme

## 4. Creative Planning Application Guidance

**Describe shot style qualitatively**, e.g.:
- "Smooth motion shots primarily, paired with refined close-ups, creating an elegant, relaxed visual feel"
- "Fast-switching multi-angle shots + handheld style, creating a tense, dynamic atmosphere"

**Describe shot combination strategy**, e.g.:
- "Product segment: wide establishing -> push in to medium -> close-up detail -> orbit full view"
- "Emotional climax: rapid scale jumps (wide -> close -> extreme close) + handheld, intensifying impact"

**Avoid**: Specifying focal lengths, exact angle parameters, shot-by-shot scripts, technical execution details
**Focus**: Overall shot style positioning, combination narrative logic, visual language matching the theme

## 5. Montage Modes (default cut logic)

Match montage type to connection logic — script-skill uses `editing-rhythm-design.md` §6 for per-beat motives.

| Mode | Default |
|------|---------|
| **Accumulation** (battle prep, training, hype build) | `action_match` + `cause_effect`; peak may add `rhythmic_beat` |
| **Parallel** (two storylines) | Alternate with contrast or `cause_effect` — not random A/B |
| **Metaphor** | `match_cut` between unlike subjects |
| **Cross-cut** (simultaneous tension) | Short pairs; second shot answers the first within two cuts |

Brainstorm marks *which beats are montage* (`pacing: montage` on those beats only). Do not plan individual cut points here — script-skill owns that.
